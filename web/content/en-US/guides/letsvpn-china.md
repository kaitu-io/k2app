## What happened: the official timeline

LetsVPN's official blog (letsvpn.world) published these notices:

| Date | Notice | What it said |
|---|---|---|
| <strong>2026-04-28</strong> | To LetsVPN users | Because of ongoing network blocking, the team had tried "almost every hour" for 20 days without solving the connection problem, and had to end its business in mainland China. Mainland payment channels were closed. ([original](https://letsvpn.world/blog/c130bd)) |
| <strong>2026-05-01</strong> | Account freeze and refund status | Refunds and compensation are calculated from <strong>April 8, 00:00</strong> (Beijing time). ([original](https://letsvpn.world/blog/dba00f)) |
| <strong>2026-05-08</strong> | Normal operations resume | Standard billing resumed for users worldwide, but "we cannot guarantee the service status in mainland China." The freeze compensation window was extended to May 12, 00:00 Beijing time. ([original](https://letsvpn.world/blog/f20bkd)) |
| <strong>2026-05-15</strong> | A letter from the CEO | "LetsVPN can no longer provide VPN service to mainland China." Mainland users were advised to process refunds as soon as possible. ([original](https://letsvpn.world/blog/h20kxb)) |

In short: LetsVPN still exists and still sells subscriptions outside China, which is why "letsvpn download" still returns its website. What it no longer promises is that the app will connect from inside mainland China.

## If you still have LetsVPN time: getting a refund

According to the May 8 notice, refund requests continue to be processed, and refunds should go through the official entry inside the app:

1. <strong>Open the LetsVPN app and use the refund entry inside it.</strong> If something is unclear, contact the in-app support.
2. <strong>Track it with the refund status page.</strong> Statuses are: received, processing, completed, or failed.

Two things to know:

- <strong>Only use the official in-app channel.</strong> LetsVPN warned that people were impersonating its staff in group chats and private messages, offering to "speed up" refunds. Anyone asking you to transfer money, share a verification code, or take remote control of your phone is running a scam.
- <strong>The refund may not match your remaining days.</strong> The CEO's letter explains that bonus time is not refunded, and long-term plans are recalculated at the price of the plan matching the time you actually used.

## How to choose a replacement: five checks

LetsVPN was a widely used app and still lost its mainland service after one round of blocking. So instead of asking "which one will never go down", ask how a provider behaves when something goes wrong:

| Check | Why it matters | How to verify |
|---|---|---|
| 1. A public, dated announcements page | When connections drop, you need to know what's going on | Look for dated release notes or announcements, with old posts still visible |
| 2. A written refund policy | If a service stops working, getting money back is what matters most | Find the refund conditions on the pricing page or in the terms |
| 3. Short plans you can test | Only your own network (home broadband, mobile data, office) can tell you if it works | Check for short plans or a refund window on the first purchase |
| 4. Published technical details | A team that documents its protocol has less room to hide problems | Look for protocol docs or technical write-ups |
| 5. A way to reach a person | When you can't connect, you need tickets or live chat that answer | Check for in-app tickets or website support |

<strong>Don't buy several years up front because it's cheap.</strong> Start with the shortest plan, use it for a week on every network you rely on, then decide.
